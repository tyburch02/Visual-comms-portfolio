Whenever I take a picture of something, it's usually with the thought, “I like this; I want to remember it, so I can immortalize it in time and revisit it whenever I want. From bathroom mirror selfies to eccentric signs to sunsets, I want to capture in real time what I see and hope that feeling translates visually. My pictures always have a sense of imperfection in them, like blurriness and odd angles, but these elements also bring a sense of human life to me. It is me experiencing life, capturing it in real time with a photo. Each imperfection is a confirmation of life being lived. The photography I make is not intended to be visually pleasing; it's just to confirm that life is being lived and actively happening to me and around me. I think I would represent our current life in a similar way: through candid, imperfect photographs of the world around me. Photography is there to document the bad, the ugly, the good, and the beautiful; the creator ultimately decides what is important for the viewer to see. While capturing grand moments such as reunions or vacations is expected, it is equally important to capture day-to-day life as it happens. The boring, slow moments of life, such as riding the train to work or doing laundry, are all activities of life passing by, and that is meaningful too. On the other hand, life is not always calm and mundane; sometimes it is chaotic, overwhelming, and unpredictable. That's why my photography would call for the blurriness, the uneven framing, and harsh lighting; it communicates the same emotions about how life can feel to all of us. My photography would show lived experience overall: that life is actively happening around us and not just to us. I would not present an idealized version of the world. Instead, I would want the viewer to confront what they see in front of them, whether it is beautiful, uncomfortable, ordinary, or chaotic. A photograph does not need to document a historically important event to say something meaningful about the time in which it was taken. Sometimes an ordinary image can tell us just as much about how people lived and what they valued. Ultimately, my work is saying, I was here; I saw this, and at this moment I decided it is worth remembering.
